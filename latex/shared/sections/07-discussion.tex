\DocSection{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\DocSubsection{Relation to policy-gradient learning}

The current practical method uses a score-function policy gradient, but its
random object is a complete deterministic policy table and its regularizer
acts on a distribution over those tables. The PPO guide initializes the
variational distribution once. Subsequent updates change its logits directly
using original rewards; fresh policies are sampled each iteration. Neither
trajectory weights nor particle genealogy are carried across updates.
The relation to REINFORCE is therefore explicit, not denied: the difference
lies in the sampled object, objective, and execution semantics.

The separately implemented MCMC alternative behaves differently. Its guide
is frozen while complete policies evolve through corrected proposals and
replica swaps; there is no guide-gradient update during that chain.
Consequently, claims about invariant distributions or MCMC mixing must not
be used as guarantees for the variational optimizer.

Determinism is a representation and execution choice, not a general
computational advantage. It makes repeated actions consistent but does not
remove stochastic transitions or the exponentially large policy space.
On the hard development instance, action resampling gives much higher goal
probability than commitment for both VI and PPO. A committed wrong action
can persist at every revisit. This is a substantive behavioral difference
and a limitation for control, not a result to hide by reporting only the
more favorable execution rule.

\DocSubsection{Approximation and interpretation}

With a uniform reference, modes of the target maximize expected return.
A finite-temperature draw need not do so. The resulting dispersion depends
on the reference and temperature; it is not calibrated epistemic uncertainty
about an unknown environment.

The direct ELBO avoids exponentiating noisy return estimates, but its
gradient can have high variance. Mean-field policies cannot express general
cross-state dependence, and reverse-KL optimization may represent one policy
region while omitting others. An unbiased estimator is a statement about
the expected update at fixed parameters, not a guarantee of global
optimization or accurate posterior coverage. Family error needs its own
small-instance characterization and behavioral tests.

Replicated SMC retains policy ancestry under an explicitly fixed-bank
approximation but can degenerate severely. The tested medium configuration
kept only a few root ancestors and almost never reached the goal. Completing
unvisited table entries makes policies syntactically distinct without
establishing useful behavioral diversity.

For MCMC, exact Hastings correction guarantees invariance, not finite-run
mixing. Local edits and even global proposals can fail to traverse
high-dimensional policy modes. Adjacent swap acceptance, return ESS,
coordinate movement and agreement between dispersed chains must therefore
be checked together. The tested bridge produced good policies but failed
to establish full distributional agreement.

\DocSubsection{Exploration and guide attribution}

Sparse rewards create a common exploration problem
\citep{andrychowiczEtAl2017}. In the hard development run, a 200k-transition
guide never achieved success, and all refinement returns were equal.
The entropy term then improved the estimated ELBO without improving control.
Full-budget assisted PPO found success only after that initial allowance.
Reallocating the same total budget toward guide learning helped in a
subsequent conditional development test. Repetition across independent
guides is required before treating this allocation as a robust choice.

This dependence on a guide is part of the method's cost and limitation.
Improvement over an under-budget initializer does not establish improvement
over full-budget PPO. Standard PPO and exploration-assisted PPO are separate
controls. In the assisted comparison, PPO retains the count bonus throughout
training, whereas VI uses it only during initialization; this distinction
must remain visible. Performance against an unassisted baseline alone
cannot isolate an inference contribution.

\DocSubsection{Access, policy class and scope}

Direct variational refinement uses only simulator returns for learning.
Exact dynamic programming is used offline for evaluation. In contrast,
exact-score MCMC uses model information during inference, and the optional
structural block builder reads exact successor probabilities even when its
scores use rollout tapes. These are separate information-access strata,
not interchangeable sample-efficiency competitors.

The fixed-bank target converges to the intended expected-return target
under finite-policy bounded-return assumptions, but finite bank size remains
an approximation. Bounded returns also permit a nonnegative Poisson
estimator for a pseudo-marginal construction; Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}
gives its identity and variance. Its potentially prohibitive cost prevents
mathematical exactness from serving as a practicality claim.

All reported output policies are stationary in physical state, while the
finite-horizon dynamic-programming upper control is nonstationary.
PPO uses Monte Carlo advantages without GAE or value clipping. Discrete SAC
uses a fixed entropy temperature and time-indexed twin critics; Double
Q-learning uses time-indexed tables before an occupancy-weighted projection
to a stationary output. These precisely specified implementations preserve
their core updates but do not claim every enhancement of the algorithm
families. Current GridWorld studies use $\gamma=1$.

Finally, the present development samples cannot establish generality or
practical equivalence. Independent training seeds and held-out maps are the
experimental units; repeated policy draws only improve conditional
evaluation precision. A nonspatial Blackjack study could add evidence at
low cost, whereas Tireworld would test structured stochastic failure with
greater implementation effort. These extensions cannot replace completing
the primary comparison and its uncertainty analysis.
