\DocSection{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experimental program begins with exact target-fidelity gates, proceeds to
small end-to-end learning tests, and only then compares methods under matched
environments and compute budgets.

\DocSubsection{Target-fidelity tests}

Small finite MDPs permit enumeration of deterministic policies and direct
calculation of Equation~\eqref{eq:policy-target}. They provide the primary
correctness tests:
\begin{itemize}
  \item total-variation or Kullback--Leibler error to the exact policy target;
  \item recovery of target modes and statewise marginals;
  \item sensitivity to transition variance at fixed expected return;
  \item invariance to random-number coupling in the large-sample limit; and
  \item agreement between memoized-policy execution and the predicted
        trajectory distribution.
\end{itemize}

\DocSubsection{Control and scaling tests}

Larger domains measure expected return, success probability, lower-tail
return, simulator calls, wall-clock time, and peak memory. Difficulty should
vary along interpretable axes: state-space size, obstacle density, horizon,
transition stochasticity, delayed reward, and number of policy modes.

Every comparison uses the same training seeds, evaluation seeds, rollout
horizon, and termination convention. Hyperparameter search budgets are
matched and reported separately from final evaluation. Reward scaling is
paired with the inverse scaling of \(\beta\) whenever the intended target is
held fixed.
