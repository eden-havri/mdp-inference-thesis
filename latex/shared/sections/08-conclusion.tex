\DocSection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Expected-return policy inference is well defined once the target measure,
reference measure, inverse temperature, and execution semantics are explicit.
The central correction is methodological: an unbiased estimator of expected
return may be used linearly in the policy-space variational objective, but it
cannot be exponentiated inside a multi-sample log average without changing the
quantity being optimized. Exact finite-policy gates and
convergence-controlled sample averages make that claim directly testable and
give the empirical results a clear interpretation.
