\DocSection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Expected-return policy inference is meaningful only after the policy space,
reference measure, inverse temperature, and execution rule are explicit.
Averaging environment randomness before exponentiation distinguishes its
target from an exponential-trajectory objective. This established Gibbs
construction motivates an implementation whose approximations can be
tested independently of its control performance.

The practical candidate uses simulator-based variational learning over
complete policy tables, initialized by a separately costed PPO guide.
Its masked score-function estimator is unbiased under the stated sampling
assumptions, and exact small-instance tests exercise the actual implementation.
It does not follow that a finite run globally optimizes the objective or
represents the full target. Corrected MCMC and fixed-bank SMC remain
documented alternatives with separate invariance, approximation and mixing
requirements.

Development results identify useful questions rather than settle the final
comparison. One rollout per policy permits more effective refinement than
four in the tested allocation. The independent-guide cluster calibration,
however, includes a medium-map collapse, and assisted PPO has higher return
in seven of eight paired comparisons. Hard-map failures and conditional
budget reallocation expose the importance of exploration before refinement.
A bounded medium-map reallocation recovers the selected failure and avoids
collapse in two fresh seeds, while their native returns still favor PPO.
The sharp difference between commitment and action resampling also prevents
equating policy consistency with improved control.

Strict total simulator caps and explicit execution semantics now support
a reproducible confirmatory study. The remaining evidence must characterize
initialization robustness, larger-instance family error, held-out performance
and full cost against the five declared baseline families. Competitive or mixed
results can support a useful policy-inference contribution; neither
universal superiority nor calibrated posterior uncertainty is claimed.
