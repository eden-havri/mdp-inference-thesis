\DocSection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Expected-return policy inference is well defined only after the policy space,
reference measure, inverse temperature, and execution semantics are explicit.
The key mathematical choice is to exponentiate a policy's expected return, not
to average exponentiated realized returns.  Guided replica-exchange
Metropolis--Hastings provides an exact sampler when policy values are computed
by dynamic programming and an exact sampler for a declared fixed-tape
sample-average target under simulator access.  Full-support guidance can
improve proposals without changing either target because every local move is
Hastings-corrected.

Bounded returns also yield a nonnegative unbiased Poisson weight for an exact
simulator-only pseudo-marginal extension.  Its explicit relative-variance
formula shows why mathematical exactness alone is insufficient: at large
inverse temperature it may require prohibitive rollout counts.  It is therefore
a gated extension, not a substitute for reporting the finite-tape approximation
honestly.

The empirical claim remains deliberately narrower than the mathematical one.
Exact tiny-domain agreement is established before scaling, and medium pilots
must demonstrate mixing and acceptable cost before any confirmatory comparison.
The final study will compare committed-policy control performance, target
fidelity, and total compute against five precisely specified baselines.  Until
those gates pass, the contribution is a testable formulation and algorithmic
protocol rather than a claim of empirical superiority.
