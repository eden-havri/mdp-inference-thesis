\DocSection{Expected-Return Policy Inference}
\label{sec:corrected-method}

\DocSubsection{Exact and sample-average policy scores}

The sampler uses a score \(\widetilde J(\policy)\).  With an explicit finite
transition model, \(\widetilde J=\ExpectedReturn_H\) is evaluated exactly by
dynamic programming.  This variant targets Equation~\eqref{eq:policy-target}
exactly, but is model-based.

With simulator-only access, draw an immutable bank of \(K\) random-number
tapes \(\Xi_K=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_K)\) and define
\begin{equation}
  \widehat J_K(\policy;\Xi_K)
  = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Return_H(\policy,\xi_k).
  \label{eq:saa-return}
\end{equation}
Conditional on that bank, the implemented target is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\beta,K}(d\policy\mid\Xi_K)
  \propto
  \exp\!\left(\beta\widehat J_K(\policy;\Xi_K)\right)\mu(d\policy).
  \label{eq:saa-target}
\end{equation}
The same tape bank is reused for every policy evaluation in a chain.  This is
both common-random-number variance reduction and a definition of the finite-
sample target.  Refreshing the bank inside an ordinary Metropolis--Hastings
ratio would generally invalidate detailed balance.  For finite policy spaces
and bounded returns, \(\widehat J_K\) converges uniformly to \(J\), so
Equation~\eqref{eq:saa-target} converges to
Equation~\eqref{eq:policy-target}; finite \(K\) is nevertheless an
approximation, not an unbiased exact-target construction.

\DocSubsection{A learned proposal that cannot change the target}

PPO-Clip \citep{schulman2017} is trained first and then frozen.  Its statewise
action probabilities guide local moves, but PPO is neither the target nor an
uncorrected component of the final sampler.  Let
\(p_\phi(a\mid s)\) be the frozen probabilities and define
\begin{equation}
  g_{\eta,\phi}(a\mid s)
  = \eta p_\phi(a\mid s)
    +(1-\eta)\frac{1}{|\mathcal A|},
  \qquad 0\leq\eta<1.
  \label{eq:mh-guide}
\end{equation}
The uniform component gives every action positive probability.  If the current
action is \(a\), a proposed different action \(a'\) has probability
\begin{equation}
  q_s(a'\mid a)
  = \frac{g_{\eta,\phi}(a'\mid s)}
         {1-g_{\eta,\phi}(a\mid s)},
  \qquad a'\neq a.
  \label{eq:forced-different-proposal}
\end{equation}
Both the guide snapshot and \(\eta\) are stored with the run.  Setting
\(\eta=0\) gives the uniform-proposal ablation.

\DocSubsection{Single-site policy Metropolis--Hastings}

One local proposal selects a decision state uniformly, changes only its action
according to Equation~\eqref{eq:forced-different-proposal}, and leaves all
other policy entries fixed.  At inverse temperature \(b\), accept
\(\policy'\) from \(\policy\) with probability
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_b(\policy,\policy')
  = \min\!\left\{1,
      \exp\!\left[b\{\widetilde J(\policy')-
                       \widetilde J(\policy)\}\right]
      \frac{\mu(\policy')}{\mu(\policy)}
      \frac{q_s(a\mid a')}{q_s(a'\mid a)}
    \right\}.
  \label{eq:local-mh}
\end{equation}
The state-selection terms cancel.  The experiments use uniform \(\mu\), so
its ratio is one.  The remaining Hastings ratio is essential because forcing a
different action makes even a fixed categorical guide asymmetric.  With full
support, this kernel is irreducible on the finite policy space and satisfies
detailed balance with the chosen exact or fixed-tape target.

\DocSubsection{Exact whole-policy independence refresh}

Single-site edits can be ineffective on a sparse-return plateau, where many
policies have the same score but reaching a useful policy requires several
coordinated action changes.  Each active replica update therefore chooses the
single-site kernel with probability \(1-\rho\), or an independence proposal
with probability \(\rho\).  The fixed whole-policy proposal is
\begin{equation}
  Q_\phi(\policy)
  =w_{\mathrm{map}}\mathbf 1\{\policy=\policy_\phi^{\mathrm{MAP}}\}
   +w_{\mathrm{guide}}\prod_{s\in\mathcal D}
       p_\phi(\policy(s)\mid s)
   +w_{\mathrm{uniform}}|\mathcal A|^{-|\mathcal D|},
  \label{eq:global-policy-proposal}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal D\) is the decision-state set and
\(\policy_\phi^{\mathrm{MAP}}\) is the statewise mode of the frozen PPO guide.
The defaults are
\(\rho=0.10\) and
\((w_{\mathrm{map}},w_{\mathrm{guide}},w_{\mathrm{uniform}})
=(0.25,0.70,0.05)\).  The components overlap, so the proposal density is the
sum in Equation~\eqref{eq:global-policy-proposal}, evaluated by log-sum-exp,
not merely the density of the component that generated the draw.  The positive
uniform component guarantees full support.

At inverse temperature \(b\), an independent draw
\(\policy'\sim Q_\phi\) is accepted with
\begin{align}
  \log R_b(\policy,\policy')={}&
      b\{\widetilde J(\policy')-\widetilde J(\policy)\}
      \notag\\
    &+\log\mu(\policy')-\log\mu(\policy)
      \notag\\
    &+\log Q_\phi(\policy)-\log Q_\phi(\policy'),
      \label{eq:global-policy-log-ratio}\\
  \alpha_b^{\mathrm{global}}(\policy,\policy')={}&
      \min\{1,\exp[\log R_b(\policy,\policy')]\}.
  \label{eq:global-policy-mh}
\end{align}
This is an ordinary independence Metropolis--Hastings step
\citep{mengersenTweedie1996}.  The complete proposal ratio is mandatory.
Consequently, the guide, its mode, and the branch probability affect efficiency
but cannot change the invariant target.  Full support gives an asymptotic
finite-space convergence guarantee, not evidence that a finite run has mixed.

Forced-different proposals can be periodic in a two-action domain.  We therefore
mix every complete within-replica-plus-swap sweep with the identity kernel:
\begin{equation}
  K_\delta=\delta I+(1-\delta)K, \qquad 0<\delta<1.
  \label{eq:lazy-sweep}
\end{equation}
The stay-put decision is independent of the chain state, so it preserves the
product target while giving every augmented chain state a positive self-transition.
The default is \(\delta=0.05\); swap parity advances only on active sweeps.
The branch settings, local and global proposal/acceptance counts, a separate
count of accepted policy-changing global moves, \(\delta\), and the realized
lazy-sweep count are stored with each run.

\DocSubsection{Replica exchange across inverse temperatures}

Single-site moves can mix slowly when good complete policies occupy separated
modes.  Replica exchange, also called parallel tempering, couples chains at
inverse temperatures
\begin{equation}
  0=b_0 < b_1 < \cdots < b_{M-1}=\beta,
  \qquad
  b_\ell^{(0)}=\beta\{\ell/(M-1)\}^p,
  \label{eq:temperature-ladder}
\end{equation}
where \(p>0\) controls the initial ladder spacing.  Development-only pilots may
replace this power ladder with a fixed, explicitly listed ladder that places
more replicas at measured exchange bottlenecks, following the feedback
principle of \citet{katzgraberEtAl2006}.  Adaptation stops before target samples
are collected, and the endpoints remain zero and \(\beta\).  Replica \(\ell\)
applies the fixed mixture of Equations~\eqref{eq:local-mh} and
\eqref{eq:global-policy-mh} with \(b=b_\ell\).  At a declared interval, four
checkerboard swap sub-sweeps are applied by default, with parity alternating
globally.  These extra swaps reuse cached values and compose invariant kernels,
so they can improve temperature-space flow without extra policy evaluations
\citep{choderaShirts2011}.  For replicas \(i<j\), the swap acceptance probability is
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{i,j}^{\mathrm{swap}}
  = \min\!\left\{1,
      \exp\!\left[(b_j-b_i)
        \{\widetilde J(\policy_i)-\widetilde J(\policy_j)\}\right]
    \right\}.
  \label{eq:swap-mh}
\end{equation}
This is a Metropolis update for the product of tempered targets
\[
  \prod_{\ell=0}^{M-1}\mu(\policy_\ell)
  \exp(b_\ell\widetilde J(\policy_\ell)),
\]
and hence preserves the desired cold marginal at \(b_{M-1}=\beta\)
\citep{geyer1991}.  The PPO guide affects only proposal efficiency;
Equations~\eqref{eq:local-mh}, \eqref{eq:global-policy-mh}, and
\eqref{eq:swap-mh} retain the target.

By default, all replicas start at the PPO statewise mode, while the
\(b_0=0\) chain is allowed to forget that initializer during burn-in.  A
separate overdispersed diagnostic initializes hotter replicas from the policy
prior.  Initialization cannot alter the stationary distribution, but it can
strongly affect a finite run.  Only thinned post-burn-in draws from the
\(b_{M-1}\) replica are reported as target samples.

\DocSubsection{Diagnostics and cost accounting}

Each run records the beta ladder, local and global proposals and acceptances,
accepted policy-changing global moves, and adjacent swap proposals and
acceptances for both the complete and post-burn windows, retained returns,
return effective sample size,
policy-value evaluations, lazy sweeps, simulator transitions, PPO training
transitions, wall-clock time, and software and source hashes.  Return effective
sample size is necessary but can miss movement between policies with similar
value.  We therefore also record conservative and quantile summaries of
action-indicator effective sample sizes, statewise switch-rate summaries,
distinct sampled policies, walker temperature visits, endpoint transitions,
round trips, and the number of walkers reaching the cold replica.  Independent
warm and overdispersed chains provide the between-chain check.  Calibration
uses two distinct decisions.  A short two-chain probe tests
only whether the proposed ladder has viable post-burn edge exchange and endpoint
flow; passing it merely permits the longer run.  The full multichain gate then
tests return \(\widehat R\) and ESS, edge exchange, cold-reaching walkers and
round trips, coordinate total variation, unique-policy fraction, mean policy
Hamming movement, and switching at nondegenerate coordinates.  Only the latter
supports a mixing claim.  A high-return cold replica cannot compensate for a
failure at either stage.

For \(I\) total iterations, \(N_{\mathrm{lazy}}\) lazy sweeps, and \(M\)
temperatures, the sampler uses
\(M+M(I-N_{\mathrm{lazy}})\) policy-value evaluations.  Swaps reuse cached values.

PPO training is part of the proposed method's cost.  Matched-budget comparisons
therefore include every interaction and optimizer update used to train the
guide plus all downstream policy evaluations.  Exact dynamic-programming
evaluations are logged separately because they consume model computations but
zero simulator transitions.

\DocSubsection{Complementary estimators and ablations}

Two additional estimators test which part of the construction matters.  First,
for a tractable distribution \(q_\theta\) over complete policies, the direct
variational objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L(\theta)
  = \beta\E_{\policy\sim q_\theta}
      [\ExpectedReturn_H(\policy)]
    - \KL(q_\theta\|\mu).
  \label{eq:correct-elbo}
\end{equation}
Sampling \(\policy\sim q_\theta\) and then a rollout conditional on that
same policy gives an unbiased estimator of the return term because it is
linear in \(J\).  A score-function contribution is
\begin{equation}
  \left(\beta\Return_H+\log\mu(\policy)
        -\log q_\theta(\policy)-c\right)
  \nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(\policy),
  \label{eq:score-gradient}
\end{equation}
where \(c\) is an action-independent control variate.

Second, replicated policy SMC instantiates each state action once in a lazy
policy memo and advances \(K\) simulator replicas under that same policy.
For newly exposed states \(U_t\), its incremental log weight is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\log w_t
  = \frac{\beta}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{t,k}
    +\sum_{s\in U_t}
      \left[\log\mu(a_s\mid s,m_{<s})
            -\log q(a_s\mid s,m_{<s})\right].
  \label{eq:guided-weight}
\end{equation}
The mean reward is formed before exponentiation, proposal correction is applied
once per newly assigned policy entry, revisits reuse the memo, and resampling
copies the entire ancestry-dependent state.  Sequential Monte Carlo can define
powerful variational families \citep{naessethEtAl2018} and particle objectives
\citep{maddisonEtAl2017}, but those results do not by themselves make an
exponentiated noisy return an unbiased estimator of
Equation~\eqref{eq:policy-target}.  Here replicated SMC is evaluated against
the explicitly stated fixed-tape target and against exact enumeration.

The isolation matrix contains uniform-guide MCMC, corrected PPO-guided MCMC,
PPO alone, direct ELBO, uniform-proposal replicated SMC, and corrected guided
SMC.  No condition may silently reuse extra simulator interactions.
