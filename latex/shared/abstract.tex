This manuscript studies inference over deterministic policies while preserving
the ordinary expected-return criterion of a stochastic Markov decision process.
The scientific target is a Gibbs distribution whose log density is proportional
to a policy's expected return, not to an exponentiated realized trajectory
return. That distinction determines which Monte Carlo estimators are valid.
The proposed approach combines a direct policy-space variational objective
with a replicated particle method that averages each policy's return over
common simulator tapes before exponentiation. Both maintain one sampled policy
across an episode. The empirical program measures control performance,
computational cost, and fidelity to the intended policy distribution.
