We study generalized Bayesian inference over complete deterministic policies
using the expected return of a stochastic finite-horizon Markov decision
process. The Gibbs target is defined before choosing an approximation:
environment randomness is averaged before the value is exponentiated.
This established policy-search formulation is distinct from weighting
individual trajectories by exponentiated realized return.

The current practical method learns a factorized distribution over complete
policy tables. A separately costed PPO guide initializes the distribution;
direct variational refinement then uses simulator returns, a leave-one-out
baseline, and an exact policy-space entropy term. We justify its
visited-state-masked score gradient and verify the actual implementation
against exact differentiation on enumerable instances. One rollout per
sampled policy is sufficient for gradient correctness, although allocation
between policy draws and repeated rollouts affects finite-budget learning.
The method samples fresh policies between updates and commits to one table
during each evaluation episode. It has no particle-resampling stage.

The empirical design separates gradient correctness, variational family
error, and control performance. Strict interaction caps include guide
training and refinement; native stochastic execution and committed-policy
execution are both reported. Preliminary GridWorld studies favor one
rollout per policy in a medium-map allocation test and find similar values
to exploration-assisted PPO in one matched-budget comparison. A failed hard
run exposes insufficient exploration; reallocating the same budget toward
guide training improves its subsequent control without eliminating the gap
between execution rules. These are development results, not confirmatory
superiority or general posterior-fidelity claims.

The thesis also documents exact-score and fixed-bank policy MCMC,
replicated SMC, their validation contracts, and unsuccessful development
configurations. The planned larger comparison includes REINFORCE, PPO-Clip,
discrete SAC, categorical cross-entropy search, and Double Q-learning.
Independent guide seeds, held-out maps, family-error analysis, and the
complete cost--performance comparison remain necessary before final conclusions.
